# rose A/B battery pack: 13S2P 21700, self-swapped onto a passive dock (mockup)

This is a concept mockup, not a released design. Everything is generated by `gen.py` (geometry) and `reach.py` (K1 arm reach check). `./build.sh` rebuilds all of it: loon → vcad → dark renders, plus `reach.txt`. Renders are slow (each pack has 26 cylinders); allow several minutes.

**Numbers marked ESTIMATE are not from a datasheet.**

## Decisions

| | decision | why |
|---|---|---|
| cell | **Molicel INR21700-P50B** (P45B as the second source) | 5 Ah *and* 60 A continuous. The only common 21700 that is both energy- and power-class |
| pack | **13S2P, 468 Wh**. The robot carries **two of them (A/B)**, so 936 Wh total = 13S4P | the robot stays powered while it swaps one pack; one envelope for robot, dock and rack UPS |
| bus | 13S NMC: 46.8 V nominal (3.6 V/cell, P50B), **54.6 V full**, 39 V empty (3.0 V/cell) | it matches the blade standard's 36–60 V input. **Your default said 48.1 V nominal; that assumes 3.7 V/cell. The P50B datasheet nominal is 3.6 V.** Either way it is the same 13S |
| placement | two **flank packs**, one in each side of the torso, either side of the blade bay; each one slides out **sideways (±X)** | it is the only placement the K1 4-DoF arm can reach without hitting its own torso (see the reach check). A front drawer misses by 53 mm or more |
| swap | concept **(c), with the robot doing the work**: the right hand pulls pack A sideways across a 6 mm gap onto a passive dock rail and up to the dock's stop. The robot side-steps to the fresh pack and pushes it home. Pack B carries the robot throughout, then the roles swap | the dock is passive (rails, pads, a charger). The robot never holds 2.7 kg at arm's length, and the pack is always supported by either receiver rails or dock rails |
| connector | **Anderson SBS 75X**: 2 power contacts (up to 110 A) + 4 mate-last/break-first auxiliary contacts, on a ±1 mm float plate with 2 Ø6 guide pins | a real hot-plug-rated family with built-in sequencing |
| BMS | on CAN: the arm controller's **free 4th CAN-FD bus**, run as classic CAN at 500 kbit/s | see the brake proposal |
| envelope | 47.4 × 116 × 226 mm body. With connector, cuff and dogs: **~118 × 116 × 238 mm** | the robot, the dock and the rack UPS all take the same pack |

**A torso change you need to accept:** the bay (109.6) plus two packs with receiver rails needs about 210 mm inside. The k1.loon shell is 206 inside, so the shell grows from **210 to 214 mm outer**. Everything else fits: packs run z 48–274 in torso-local coordinates (inside 46–280), and the connector sits below the bay (bay z ≥ 122).

The M50LT is an energy cell, at 0.29 A per mAh of continuous current. In 2P it gives 14.6 A per pack, which would trip on any leg transient, so it's out. The 50S works only if you accept the thermal cut-off at our 29 A per cell worst case. The P45B is a drop-in at 90% of the energy.

## Power budget and runtime (ESTIMATE)

| load | average | peak |
|---|---|---|
| legs (K1-class, carrying ~15 kg of arms) | 250 W | 1.5 kW |
| two rose arms | 2 × 50 W | 2 × 200 W (each arm controller is capped at 40 A ≈ 1.9 kW) |
| compute blades (bay of 4, 20 W max each) | 60 W | 80 W |
| sensors, head, radios | 25 W | 30 W |
| conversion losses ~6% | 26 W | |
| **total** | **~460 W** | **~2.0 kW realistic, 45 A at a 44 V sag** |

- **Usable energy:** 936 Wh × 0.90 (DoD) × 0.95 (cold and aging) ≈ **800 Wh**.
- **Runtime:** **≈ 1.7 h** of walking litter work, or **≈ 2.2 h** of mostly standing pick work (legs ~150 W). That is just short of the 2 h target when walking. A swap takes about a minute of robot time and leaves the robot powered, so runtime is set by dock capacity rather than the pack.
- **Worst-case current ceiling:** both arm controllers at their 40 A limit plus legs at 34 A ≈ **116 A**. That is 58 A per pack and 29 A per cell (P50B: 60 A). One pack alone, during a swap with the robot standing still, sees ~200 W ≈ 5 A.

## Pack options

P50B for all rows. Pack mass ESTIMATE = cells × 1.43, from this pack's breakdown: cells 1.85 kg, holders 0.12, busbars 0.10, BMS 0.08, case 0.35, connector + latch + fuse 0.15.

| option | cells | Wh | cells kg | pack kg | cell-limited A | A/cell at 116 A | cell volume | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13S3P | 39 | 702 | 2.8 | ~3.9 | 180 | 39 | 1.0 L | 1.3 h; no swap redundancy |
| 13S4P | 52 | 936 | 3.7 | ~5.2 | 240 | 29 | 1.35 L | right energy, but one 5 kg brick: the robot goes dark to swap it, and the arm can't lift it |
| 13S6P | 78 | 1404 | 5.5 | ~7.8 | 360 | 19 | 2.0 L | doesn't fit the torso next to the bay |
| **2 × 13S2P (A/B)** | 2 × 26 | 2 × 468 = **936** | 2 × 1.85 | **2 × 2.65** | 2 × 120 | 29 | 2 × 0.67 L | **chosen** |

**Chosen pack:** 13S2P, 468 Wh, **~2.65 kg (ESTIMATE)**. The body is 47.4 × 116 × 226 = 1.24 L; about 1.6 L gross with the connector and cuff. Peak current: 120 A cell-limited, 110 A at the connector, 100 A fused, against ~58 A per-pack worst case and ~23 A realistic.

**Why A/B wins for self-swap:**
- One pack is always live, so there is no shutdown, reboot, or fall risk mid-swap.
- Each pack is light enough to slide by hand (μ ≈ 0.2 × 26 N ≈ 5 N of friction).
- Each pack fits a 47 mm flank column, which is the only reachable place.
- **The cost:** two BMSs, two connectors and paralleling logic. During a swap the robot has half the peak power, so it must stand still.

## Self-swap mechanism

![swap 1](swap_1.png) ![swap 2](swap_2.png) ![swap 3](swap_3.png) ![swap 4](swap_4.png)

1. **Hook** (`swap_1`): the robot stands beside the dock, with dock slot 1 empty and level with its right receiver. The right hand-ball enters pack A's **ball cuff** (Ø47 seat; low, in the front third of the flank). Seating the ball presses the release paddle:
   - the cam retracts the top and bottom latch dogs;
   - the handle microswitch opens the presence loop, so pack A's BMS opens its FETs before anything moves.
2. **Draw** (`swap_2`): the hand pulls +X and slides the pack 71 mm out of the receiver, across a 6 mm gap, onto the dock rails and against the dock stop. The stop's cam opens the cuff fingers and the ball releases. The floor pads land on the dock's pogo pins, and charging starts after a CAN handshake.
3. **Step** (`swap_3`): the robot side-steps 150 mm to slot 2 and hooks the fresh pack. Its cuff fingers close on the ball and the dock stop releases it.
4. **Seat** (`swap_4`): the hand pushes the pack −X into the receiver.
   - The funnel captures it, the rails guide it, and the guide pins align it.
   - In the last 8 mm the latch cam draws it home, so the hand only needs ~20 N against the connector's insertion force.
   - The receiver's mouth cam opens the cuff fingers and the hand withdraws.
   - The aux contacts make last, the presence loop closes, the BMS wakes, and precharge runs.
   - Then the left hand does pack B, the same way, from the robot's left side.

### Reach (K1 arm, `reach.py` → `reach.txt`)

The chain is from `rose/mini/cad/k1.loon`: shoulder at (±132, 0, 254) in torso-local coordinates, then shoulder pitch, shoulder roll, elbow, wrist yaw. The upper arm is 180 mm, and it is 189 mm from the elbow to the hand-ball centre (369 mm total). I used k1.loon's joint limits. The check is a brute-force search on a 2° grid, rejecting any pose where a link intersects the torso or pelvis (inflated by the link radius).

**Axis caveat:** k1.loon puts shoulder "pitch" and the elbow on its Y axis. With the shoulders on ±X, that swings the arm *sideways* and folds the forearm away from the body. I used the physical convention instead: pitch and elbow about the lateral axis, roll about the fore-aft axis, with the same limit values. **k1.loon's joint axes are worth fixing.**

- **Front-drawer packs are unreachable.**
  - The right hand misses a chest handle by 53–54 mm, because the elbow is at its −120° limit.
  - The left hand (cross-body) misses by 213 mm. Shoulder roll allows only 15° of adduction.
- **The flank grip is reachable.** The grip is the ball-cuff centre at x 134, y −35, z 56: 8 mm above the pack floor, 29 mm proud of the flank, in the front third.
  - Along the whole 71 mm draw to the dock stop (x 134 → 205), the pose is found within the grid resolution, and every joint stays **6–8° inside its limit**. Shoulder pitch sits at 50–52° of its 60° backward limit, and the elbow at −108…−114° (limit −120°).
  - A ±10 mm box around the seated grip is reachable with **2–10°** of margin. The weak direction is +Z.
- **Better option:** drop the packs 36 mm (grip at z 20, into the pelvis transition). The margin then rises to **15–18°**. It costs a taller lower receiver and a redesign of the pelvis top, so I've left it as an open question.
- **The rose arm (731 mm reach)** has ample margin; I didn't model its mount.

## Connector and latch

**Connector: Anderson SBS 75X** ([PEI-Genesis](https://www.peigenesis.com/en/anderson-power/multipole-connectors/sbs/sbs75x-upto-110.html), [Mouser](https://www.mouser.ca/anderson-power-products-sbs50-sbs75)):

- 2 power contacts, up to **110 A** (one listing says 105 A), rated 600 V. All positions are rated for hot-plug interruption.
- Up to 4 **mate-last/break-first** aux contacts (20 A) in four selectable lengths.
- Anderson does not spec blind-mate float, so the receiver mounts the socket on a **±1 mm float plate** behind the two guide pins.
- Use the low-detent contacts. **Insertion force is ESTIMATED at 40–60 N**; measure it before you trust the 20 N hand number.

| contact | function | sequence |
|---|---|---|
| power + / − | pack terminals (dead until the BMS enables) | **first make, last break** |
| aux 1, aux 2 | **PRES loop** (below) | **last make, first break** (shortest pins) |
| aux 3, aux 4 | CAN H / L. CAN ground is the pack negative, already made | mid length |

**Latch:**
- Two spring dogs (top and bottom) on a cam bar inside the outer wall, engaging strikes in the receiver.
- **Release:** seat the ball in the cuff. The paddle needs ~15 N over 5 mm of pre-travel. No human and no dock actuator is involved; the pack can only be released by the hand that is holding it.
- **Insert:** the dogs are ramped and snap in, and the cam draws the last 8 mm.
- **Retention ESTIMATE:** each dog holds ≥ 300 N, so a fall or a 10 g side shock on a 2.65 kg pack (260 N) stays latched. The cuff paddle can't be pressed by inertia: the paddle is ~10 g against a 15 N spring.
- The flank receiver is low and on the sides, so a forward fall lands on the front shell, not the cuff.

### Hot-swap, precharge and paralleling

- **Pack side:** the BMS keeps the discharge and charge FETs open unless the PRES loop carries current. A loose pack's terminals are dead.
- **Insert:** the power contacts make dead, then the aux contacts close the loop. The BMS compares the pack to the bus:
  - **|ΔV| < 0.5 V:** it closes both FETs. The packs run in parallel bidirectionally and share regen.
  - **pack > bus + 0.5 V:** it runs discharge-only, through the body diode and an ideal-diode controller with a 2 A soft-start, until the two packs equalize, then closes fully.
  - **pack < bus − 0.5 V:** the charge FET stays open. The pack joins once the live pack has discharged down to it. Packs are never cross-charged at full current.
- **Inrush:** the bus is already live from the other pack, so the receiver's precharge only covers bus-capacitance mismatch. It is a 2 A current-limited soft-start on the receiver backplane (LM5069-class), up to 100 ms. With no pack in either side (cold start on the dock), the arm controllers' own LM5060 inrush control covers their bulk capacitance.
- **Over CAN** the BMS reports SoC, SoH, pack voltage, current, cell min and max, temperatures, **charge-current limit (charge acceptance)** and **discharge-current limit**, all at 10 Hz. Plus fault flags, the latch state and the serial number.

### PRES pin, electrically (for the arm controller's e-stop/enable chain)

- **Type:** a **dry contact closure** in the pack. It needs no pack-side supply, so a sleeping or dead pack still reads as present-or-absent correctly.
- **Pack side:** in series between aux 1 and aux 2:
  - the handle microswitch (closed when latched and the cuff paddle is not pressed);
  - an optocoupler LED with 2.2 kΩ, which wakes the BMS and tells it to enable.
- **Receiver side:** 12 V through 2.2 kΩ, giving ≈ 2.5 mA of loop current.
  - A comparator or opto senses it and outputs **open-drain `PACK_x_OK`**, active-low = present and latched, pulled up to 3.3 V on the arm controller.
  - Logic thresholds: < 0.8 V = OK, > 2.0 V = absent. A 1 ms RC filter.
- **What it gates:**
  1. that pack's BMS output FETs (pack side);
  2. that receiver's soft-start/ORing controller;
  3. the arm controller's enable chain as **`EN = ESTOP_OK AND (PACK_A_OK OR PACK_B_OK)`**. Losing *one* pack must not e-stop the arms; losing both must.
  4. Each `PACK_x_OK` also goes to an MCU GPIO, so firmware **derates the arm current limit to 50%** while only one pack is present.
- **Break-first timing:** the paddle's 5 mm of pre-travel at ≤ 0.1 m/s gives ≥ 50 ms between the loop opening and the power contacts moving. The BMS opens its FETs in < 10 ms, so **the power contacts never break under load**.

## Brake interaction: proposal for the arm controller

**The problem:** `arm/controller/DESIGN.md` sets the brake chopper on at **53.3 V** and off at **50.0 V** (R16/R20 = 309k over R17 = 10.2k). A full 13S pack sits at **54.6 V**, so the brake would latch on at rest and dump 16.5 A (≈ 900 W) into the 3.3 Ω foot resistor until the pack drained to 50 V.

The prose in DESIGN.md §power still says 53.5/50.5 V. That should be reconciled to the table.

**Options:**

| option | for | against |
|---|---|---|
| A. battery-aware hardware thresholds | works with CAN dead, MCU hung or pack FETs open (the worst case: regen with no battery to absorb it) | a fixed threshold can't know that a full or cold pack *can't accept* the regen it would let through |
| B. brake gated on BMS charge-acceptance over CAN | knows the real limit (full, cold, BMS charge FET open) | fails unsafe if CAN is lost, unless it falls back to hardware anyway |
| C. go to 12S (50.4 V full) | no divider change | doesn't fix anything: the brake would still let the bus rise to 53.3 V = 4.44 V/cell before firing. You lose 8% of the energy and break the shared 13S envelope |

**Recommendation: A in hardware as the floor, plus B in firmware, which may only ever add braking.**

1. **Hardware comparator: on 57.0 V / off 55.5 V.**
   - Off must stay above full charge, worst case: with ±1.2% divider and reference tolerance, off ≥ 54.8 V > 54.6 V. So the brake can never latch on a resting full pack.
   - On must stay above off plus a 1.5 V hysteresis band: worst case ≤ 57.7 V.
   - The comparator hardware stays as it is. Scale the divider so that the on threshold ratio rises by 57.0 / 53.3 = 1.069, and shrink the hysteresis from 3.3 V to 1.5 V. The arm team is redoing the resistor values.
   - At 57.7 V the 3.3 Ω resistor sinks 17.5 A, which is more than the 10 A continuous arm envelope. The resistor's 200 W is pulse duty; that's unchanged.
2. **Firmware (MCU forces the brake), add-only:**
   - Use the **lower** of the BMS-reported charge-current limits across the present packs.
   - **Soft brake on at 55.0 V / off at 54.4 V** when either:
     - the total charge-current limit is less than the regen current the controller predicts, which covers full, cold (< 0 °C, limit 0) and BMS charge FET open; or
     - the BMS data is stale (> 200 ms).
   - Otherwise there is no soft brake, and regen goes into the packs.
   - Firmware can force the brake **on**. It can never inhibit the hardware comparator.
3. **TVS: move from SMDJ58A to SMDJ60A.**
   - The SMDJ58A is V_WM 58 V, V_BR ≥ 64.4 V, V_C 93.6 V at I_PP ([Littelfuse via RS](https://uk.rs-online.com/web/p/tvs-diodes/8768161), [WeEn](https://admin3w.ween-semi.com/en/product/SMDJ58A)). With the new brake-on worst case of 57.7 V, plus ripple while braking, the bus would sit at its stand-off voltage. It wouldn't conduct (that starts around 64 V), but stand-off is meant to be ≥ the maximum working voltage.
   - **SMDJ60A:** V_WM 60 V, V_BR ≥ 66.7 V. Its V_C is ≈ 96.8 V at the full 10/1000 µs I_PP, **from memory; check the datasheet**. That leaves only ~3% under the 100 V FETs at full rated surge, though real bus transients are far below I_PP.
   - If the power-path sim shows V_C closer to 100 V, keep the SMDJ58A and accept stand-off ≈ working voltage instead. Don't go to SMDJ64A: its V_C ≈ 103 V exceeds the FET rating.
4. **CAN: put the BMS on the free 4th CAN-FD bus (MCP251863), not the uplink.**
   - Run it as a dedicated body **power bus**, classic CAN at 500 kbit/s; BMS ICs and off-the-shelf BMS boards speak classic CAN, and the FD controller accepts it.
   - Daisy-chain it through pack A, pack B, both arm controllers and the leg power board, with 120 Ω at the two receiver backplanes.
   - Why not the uplink: brake soft-gating gets fixed latency that motion traffic can't starve, a babbling uplink can't hide a pack fault, and the uplink stays free for commands.

## Dock and rack UPS (`dock_iso`)

- **Robot dock:** a pair of rail channels at receiver height (world z ≈ 550), with the lead-in, a stop with a cam that opens the cuff, floor pogo pads, and a charger under it. It is passive apart from the charger.
- **Rack UPS: a 3U, 10" shelf holding 3 packs on their side** (3 × 468 = 1.4 kWh, 72 mm pitch, 216 mm of the 222.25 mm opening). The floor pads face a rear pogo backplane that carries the chargers and ideal-diode ORing onto the rack's 48 V bus.
  - It is **3 packs, not 4**: the side-mounted SBS 75X and the cuff stick out of the 47.4 mm body.
  - Upright packs would need 6U, and a pack lying connector-rearward doesn't fit the 222 mm opening. So **the dock and the UPS use the floor pads (4 × spring contacts: CHG+, CHG−, CANH, CANL; ~15 A, ESTIMATE), not the SBS**.
  - At ~1 kW of rack load, 3 packs give 5.2 A each on the pads, and **~1.2 h of UPS runtime (ESTIMATE)**.
  - The pads stay dead until the dock completes a CAN handshake with the BMS.
- **Charging:** the rack PSUs are 48/54 V CRPS units, so each slot needs a CC/CV charger at 54.6 V and 5 A (0.5 C, 273 W; buck-boost from 48 V). That takes ~2 h from empty.

## Safety and regulatory

- **Fusing:**
  - per cell, a nickel fuse-link in the busbar (≈ 20–30 A melt, ESTIMATE; size it to the busbar geometry);
  - per pack, a **100 A DC fuse rated ≥ 80 V DC** at the pack negative. The part number is TBD; it must interrupt the pack's short-circuit current, ~13 × 4.2 V / (2P × ~15 mΩ) ≈ 1.6 kA, ESTIMATE;
  - the BMS short-circuit trip at 200 A / 200 µs;
  - receiver-side, the 40 A arm controller limits already exist.
- **Cell spacing and holders:**
  - 0.8 mm web holders (22.5 mm pitch), with a 1.5 mm plate each side of every tier;
  - **no cell-to-cell contact**;
  - V-0 PC holders, with a gap pad only on the inner face.
- **Venting and thermal runaway path:**
  - The cells vent at their + cap. The busbars are slotted so gas can flow.
  - Gas goes **out through a burst disc on the pack's outer face**, flank-outward, away from the bay and the other pack.
  - A **mica barrier** lines the pack's inner face and the receiver's inner wall, between the pack and the bay.
  - Tiers are separated by holder plates. Propagation testing is still owed (UL 2580-style single-cell trigger).
  - A torso NTC per receiver and the BMS cell temperatures are reported over CAN; firmware sits the robot down and **pulls the pack onto the nearest flat surface** if a pack exceeds 70 °C. That is a behaviour, ESTIMATE.
- **UN38.3:** the pack is 468 Wh, which is over the 100 Wh threshold, so it **ships as Class 9 UN3480** (alone) or **UN3481** (in the robot). The design must pass UN38.3 T1–T8 on the *pack*, with a test summary. The cells (P50B) carry their own UN38.3.
- **IP rating for street use:**
  - target **IP54 for the robot with packs seated**, and **IP67 for the pack alone** (gasketed case, sealed burst disc, potted BMS);
  - the SBS 75X is not sealed, so the receiver mouth needs a lip seal around the pack body;
  - the floor pads are exposed but dead, and are gold over nickel.

## Open questions

1. **Torso width +4 mm** (210 → 214). Is that OK, or do the bay walls double as receiver walls?
2. **Runtime is 1.7 h walking vs a 2 h target.** Do we accept it, given hot swap, or does the leg budget come down?
3. **K1 reach margin is only 6–8°, and 2° at +10 mm Z.** Should the packs drop 36 mm (margin 15–18°), or do we plan on the rose arm? Also, k1.loon's shoulder and elbow axes should be fixed.
4. SBS 75X insertion force with low-detent contacts. Measure it; the 20 N hand number depends on the cam draw.
5. The paralleling policy (bidirectional within 0.5 V) needs a sim of a live insert at a 4 V mismatch.
6. Fuse part, pad contacts and the burst-disc part are all TBD.
7. Litter docks: how many slots per robot, and is dock-side charging at 0.5 C enough for the duty cycle?
8. The K1's own Booster battery: is it removed on rose, or kept as a third source?
